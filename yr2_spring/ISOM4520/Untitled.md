The conditional expectations and variance treat known variables as constant and nothing else. One important property is the tower property which is especially helpful for simplifying formula containing variables after being taken conditional expected value become constant or even zero. for example Var(R_t)=E(sigma_t^2). It also implies that if sigma_t^2 is stationary (non constant and thus conditionally heteroscedastic), E(sigma_t^2) is obviously constant and thus R_t is unconditionally homoscedastic. Here, we use GARCH to model the stochastic volatility (sigma_t^2). In GARCH, the conditional variance is a linear combination of a constant, yesterday's realized volatility, and previous best estimate of yesterday volatility. The unconditional (long run) variance can be solved by taking expectation of both side, with which we can rewrite the GARCH equation as a weighted average of the long-run variance, yesterday's realized volatility, and previous best estimate of yesterday volatility. We can run simulation by setting parameters and the underlying distribution for the standardized return $z$ to generate return prediction based on historic unconditional variance. We can also fitting historic returns to GARCH to solve for each parameter. Since our goal is to predict the future volatility, we find that the best prediction is the conditional expectation under the minimizing MSE definition. Now we can deduce the k-step ahead prediction by taking conditional expectation on GARCH and do recurrsion to find the explicit formula and plot the graph.
