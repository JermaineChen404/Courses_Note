## Step 1: Need Recognition
- understand consumers' needs
	- trigger a need/want
	- provide a solution

## Step 2: Information Search

- more alternative are not always better

## Step 3: Evaluation of Alternatives
After arriving at a set of final brand choices, consumer uses the information to evaluate alternative brands in the choice set.

## Step 4: Purchase Decisions
$$\text{Purchase Intention} \neq \text{Purchase Decision}$$
due to
- attitudes of others
- unexpected situational factors

## Step 5: Postpurchase Behavior
Postpurchase customer satisfaction is key to building profitable customer relationships

Cognitive dissonance: buyer discomfort cased by postpurchase conflict.
- Manage customer expectation
- conduct consumer satisfaction survey

# Types of Buying Decision Behavior
![[Pasted image 20250930154531.png]]

## Complex Buying Behavior
Expensive, risky, infrequently, and highly self-expressive purchases
- Require detailed info
- e.g., car, house, computer

## Dissonance-Reducing Buying Behavior
Expensive, infrequent, and risky purchases with little difference among brands
- Vulnerable to postpurchase dissonance
	- require after-sale communication

## Variety-Seeking Buying Behavior
Market leaders: encourage habitual buying behavior
- dominate shelf space
- run frequent reminder ads

Challenger firms: encourage variety-seeking
- offer special deals, coupons, samples

## Habitual Buying Behavior
- price and sales promotions
- add new features to differentiate

### Perception
- Selective attention:
	- screen out most of the information to which they are exposed
- Selective distortion
	- interpret information in a way that will support what they already believe
- Selective retention
	- remember good points made about a brand they favor

Situational Factors
- Compromise effect
	- avoid extremes
- Attraction effect (asymmetric dominance effect)
	- 第三选项 C 被称为“诱导选项”或“诱饵”，它的存在让 A 显得更合理、更值得选择，从而影响消费者的最终决策。
- Default choices
	- tendency to go for default choice
- Framing effects
	- 描述上更好听但本质等价的选择

# Business Market
Differences with consumer markets
- Market structure and demand
	- Fewer and larger buyers

		- demand not much affected by price changes
	- Fluctuating demand
		- A small percentage increase in consumer demand can cause a large increase in demand for plants and equipment
	- Derived demand
		- The demand for business goods is ultimately derived from the demand for consumer goods.
