# L3
First note that the amount of futures that can be traded on any commodity is not restricted in any way by the actual amount of this commodity that exists in the world and can be delivered at the expiration of the futures. In fact, most often the amount of futures of any kind vastly exceeds the amount of the underlying commodity.

Now think of a situation where the long side of a futures contract can demand physical delivery of the underlying at expiration (this is true in many cases).

Assume also that you have vast financial resources, or have access to vast funding from banks and other lenders. ”Vast” here means ”comparable to the value of the total amount” of an underlying commodity (at current prices) that is available for delivery against futures written on this commodity.

If the futures exchange does not impose any quantitative restrictions on your trading, how can you ensure that you make a profit?

>[!Success] Short Squeeze
>### Step 1: Identify a Suitable Commodity
>Choose a commodity where the physical deliverable supply is limited relative to the open interest in futures contracts. This is crucial because if the futures market has a larger number of contracts than the available physical commodity, short sellers may struggle to fulfill delivery obligations. Commodities with low storage costs or inelastic supply are often preferred for such strategies.
> ### Step 2: Accumulate a Large Long Position in Futures Contracts
>Using your vast resources, begin acquiring a significant portion of the long positions in the futures market for this commodity. Focus on contracts nearing expiration (e.g., the front month or delivery month) to maximize pressure on short sellers. Since the futures exchange imposes no quantitative restrictions on trading, you can accumulate contracts without limits, potentially becoming the dominant long holder.
>### Step 3: Demand Physical Delivery at Expiration
>As the futures contracts approach expiration, exercise your right as the long holder to demand physical delivery of the underlying commodity. This is a standard feature in many futures contracts, and you insist on taking delivery rather than closing out positions through cash settlement or offsetting trades.
>### Step 4: Create a Short Squeeze
>- Short sellers (the counterparties to your long positions) are now obligated to deliver the physical commodity. However, because the total open interest in futures exceeds the available physical supply, many short sellers will be unable to procure enough of the commodity to meet all delivery demands. This triggers a short squeeze:
>- Short sellers panic and attempt to buy back their short positions to avoid defaulting on delivery. Since you control a large portion of the long contracts, they must buy from you or other long holders at increasingly higher prices.
>- The buying pressure from short covering drives up the price of both the futures contracts and the physical commodity. As the price rises, you can sell your long positions at a profit without ever taking delivery, or you can partially sell to lock in gains.
>### Step 5: Profit from the Price Increase
>You can realize profits in several ways:
>- **Sell Futures Contracts:** As prices surge due to the short squeeze, you sell your long futures contracts at higher prices than your purchase price, generating a direct profit.
>- **Take Delivery and Sell Physical:** If you choose to take delivery, you can hold the physical commodity and sell it in the spot market at inflated prices, further increasing profits. With control over a significant portion of the physical supply, you can also manipulate spot prices to your advantage.
>- **Roll Positions:** If the squeeze extends over multiple contract months, you can roll your positions into later months, maintaining pressure and continuing to profit.
    
This is exactly what happened in the [[Oct 13#Silver Short Squeeze|Silver short squeeze on Oct 13th]]
