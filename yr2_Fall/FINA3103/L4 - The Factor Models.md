# The Factor Models
## CAPM Limitations
Recalling [[L3 - Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM)|CAPM]],  we assume

- **linear** relation between market and stock excess returns
	- not true in historic data
- only $\beta_{x}=\frac{\sigma_{xm}}{\sigma_{m}^2}$ is relevant in return determinations
	- Intuitions tell us other factors are also relevant

## Multiple Factors Model
- The small firm effect (Keim 1981)
- The book-to-market effect (Stattman 1980, Rosenberg et al. 1985)
- The momentum effect (Jegadeesh and Titman 1993)

### Size anomaly
>[!Note] Empirical fact
>Small stocks (low market cap) have higher returns than large stocks (high market cap).

![[Pasted image 20251106003601.png]]

>[!Example] Explanations
> >[!tip] Rational risk view $\to$ Hold
> >The implied risks of small companies is greater, and thus the return is adjusted to compensate the risk premium.
> >- small companies are more vulnerable to recession due to insufficient recourses and credits
>
> >[!warning] Mispricing view $\to$ Buy
> > small companies are unknown, and often ignored by investors, so they are undervalued by the market.
